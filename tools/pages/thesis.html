How it works

Good businesses usually fix something people have put up with for years. The job is to pin that down exactly, then build the smallest real business that fixes it.

  1. Be specific about the problem

    Not “small businesses need better software”. The exact step: typing roster hours into payroll twice, answering a carbon question in a tender, ringing three installers for a price.

    SMEasy · ZerraLedger · BatteryMe

  2. Tie it to something that won’t go away

    A rule, a rebate, a public dataset, a change in what big buyers have to report. That keeps the problem real after the hype dies down.

    Scope 3 reporting · battery rebate · CER public data

  3. Use AI to do more with less

    AI tools do a lot of the heavy lifting in design, code and testing. That’s how one small outfit builds and runs several products, and why they don’t have to cost what the big software companies charge.

    {{ventureCount}} products, one studio

  4. Ship something people can use

    Real users and real data from the first release, and charge as soon as it’s worth paying for. A business earns more work by being used.

    Stripe live on SMEasy, 17 Nov 2026

  5. Keep shipping

    Small updates, every week, written down. The ledger on the home page is the record.

    Studio Ledger, on the home page
